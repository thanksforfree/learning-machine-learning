Embedding matrix E: lookup ka simple trick

Maan lo vocabulary 10,000 words ki hai aur embedding size 300. Matrix E ka shape 300 × 10,000 ho sakta hai. Har column ek word ka embedding.

E · o_j = e_j
  • E = embedding matrix, shape 300 × 10,000.
  • o_j = word j ka one-hot vector, length 10,000.
  • e_j = word j ka 300-number embedding.

One-hot multiply mathematically column lookup hai. Agar o_j mein index 42 pe 1 hai, E·o_j simply E ka 42nd column nikaal deta hai. Practice mein libraries direct lookup karti hain; full multiply nahi.

Neural language model se embeddings seekhna

Ek tareeka: pichhle kuch words lo, unki embeddings nikalo, neural network ko bolo next word predict karo. Backprop se output weights ke saath embedding matrix E bhi update hota hai.

Context words→Embedding lookup→Neural net→Next word softmax

Example: “aaj garam ___” ke context se target “chai”. Model galat predict karega, loss aayega, gradients E ke columns ko adjust karenge. “chai” similar contexts mein aane wale words ke paas aane lagta hai.

Skip-gram: context → target

Skip-gram mein center/context word se nearby target word predict karte hain. Agar sentence hai “garam chai piyo”, center “chai” se target “garam” ya “piyo” ban sakta hai. Training pairs bahut ban jaate hain.

Problem: target choose karne ke liye 10,000-word softmax lagao, har pair pe 10,000 scores. Dataset million pairs ka ho toh bill lamba.

P(target | context) = softmax(score for all vocab words)
  • Softmax saare vocabulary words compare karta hai.
  • 10k vocab matlab har example pe 10k output scores.
  • Badi vocabulary mein ye expensive ho jaata hai.

Hierarchical softmax ka zikr

Ek shortcut: words ko tree mein arrange karo. 10,000 words ko flat compare karne ke bajay tree path follow karo. Roughly log₂(10,000) ≈ 14 decisions lag sakte hain, 10,000 se kaafi kam. Bas tree design aur implementation ka apna complexity hai.

Context sample kaise: “the”, “ka” jaise shabd kam

Corpus mein common words bahut zyada aate hain: “the”, “ka”, “hai”. Agar unko equally training pairs banane do, model ka time common fillers pe kharch ho jaata hai. Isliye frequent words ko downsample karte hain — kabhi-kabhi skip kar dete hain — taaki informative words zyada sunai dein.

Intuition: Agar class mein ek student har 2 second “sir sir” bol raha hai, teacher kabhi-kabhi ignore karega taaki baaki meaningful sawaal bhi sun sake.

Negative sampling: 1 sahi + k galat

Full softmax ke bajay simple binary games banao. Context “chai” diya. Positive target “garam” hai: label 1. Phir random negative words “metro”, “pencil”, “raja” lo: label 0. Model k+1 binary classifiers train karta hai.

P(label=1 | context, word) = sigmoid(θ_wordᵀ e_context)
  • e_context = context word embedding.
  • θ_word = candidate output word ka parameter.
  • Har sampled word ke liye binary yes/no.

Ab har pair pe 10,000 outputs nahi; sirf 1 positive + k negatives. k small dataset pe 5–20, large pe 2–5 jaise choices practical ho sakti hain.

Negatives f(w)^{3/4} ke hisaab se

Negative words pure uniform choose karoge toh rare weird words bahut aa sakte hain. Pure frequency se choose karoge toh “ka”, “hai” hi baar-baar. Word2Vec style compromise: probability ∝ f(w)3/4. Frequency ko soften kar deta hai.

Dimaag mein aaye sawaal

E ka shape 300 × 10,000 kyun, 10,000 × 300 kyun nahi?

Convention depend karta hai. Formula E·o_j mein one-hot column vector hai, isliye E 300 × 10,000 rakha. Libraries internally transpose style use kar sakti hain.

Negative word sach mein context mein aa sakta ho toh?

Ho sakta hai, sampling noisy hoti hai. Large data mein noise average out hota hai. Objective perfect label dataset nahi, useful embeddings seekhne ka shortcut hai.

Hierarchical softmax aur negative sampling dono use karte hain?

Aksar ek choose karte hain. Dono full softmax ko efficient banane ke alternatives hain.

✍️ Ab tumhari baari (pehle khud socho, phir answer kholna)

Embedding size 50 aur vocab 20,000 hai. E·o_j convention mein E ka shape?

E rows embedding size, columns vocab. Shape = 50 × 20,000.

1 positive aur k=4 negatives hain. Ek training context ke liye kitne binary examples?

1 + 4 = 5 binary examples.

Frequent words ko downsample kyun karte hain?

Taaki “ka”, “hai” jaise common words training pairs ko dominate na karein, aur informative words ko chance mile.
